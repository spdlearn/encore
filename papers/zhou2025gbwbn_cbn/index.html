Bures-Wasserstein and Cholesky Batch Normalizations Maintain Finite Gradients at Condition Number $10^{6}$

Paper: Learning to Normalize on the SPD Manifold under Bures-Wasserstein and Cholesky Geometries (CVPR 2025 / IEEE TNNLS 2025) · Authors: Tao Zhou, Rui Wang, Ziheng Chen, Xiao-Jun Wu · Geometry: Generalized Bures-Wasserstein Barycenter & Log-Cholesky Tangent Standardization

Original Paper / Code ↗ · Raw Trackio Logbook JSON · Trackio Markdown · Branch: feat/pr12-armagnac-gbwbn

All Paper Datasets: Closed-Loop spd-bench & MOABB Harness Audit (4 Datasets)

How do Generalized Bures-Wasserstein BatchNorm (GBWBN) and Cholesky BatchNorm (CBN) compare when normalizing ill-conditioned SPD batches and decoding cross-session EEG? To test whether CBN standardizes Log-Cholesky tangent coordinates and whether GBWBN remains stable at condition number $10^{6}$, we audit SPDBatchNormCholesky and SPDBatchNormBW. First, SPDBatchNormCholesky(rebias=False) standardizes the Log-Cholesky tangent batch to zero mean (max error 6.48e-16) and unit variance (max error 4.06e-5; Panel A, Claim C1). Second, on SPD batches with condition number kappa = $10^{6}$, SPDBatchNormBW($\theta = 0.5$, n_iter=1) maintains 100% finite backward gradients (Panel B, Claim C2). Third, on real BNCI2014-001 Subject 3 cross-session transfer, SPDNet equipped with SPDBatchNormBW achieves 69.44% test accuracy and SPDBatchNormCholesky achieves 66.67% (Panel C, Claim C3). Overall, these results demonstrate that both positive-curvature Bures-Wasserstein centering and Cholesky tangent standardization provide numerically stable alternatives to AIRM batch normalization.
