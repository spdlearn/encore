Do Riemannian SPD neural networks reproduce their mathematical, signal, and benchmark claims?

Deep neural networks on symmetric positive definite ($\mathcal{S}_{++}^d$) and correlation ($\mathcal{C}_{++}^d$) manifolds promise invariance to linear mixing, electrode gain scaling, and cross-session drift, yet published studies evaluate heterogeneous preprocessing pipelines and uncalibrated baselines. To test whether 26 foundational and recent SPD architectures (2017 to 2026) satisfy their stated claims under a unified protocol, we extract 78 falsifiable anchor claims across Structural, Signal, and Analytical tiers, execute them on the MOABB BNCI2014-001 (NEMAR nm000139) and spd-bench (HDM05, Radar, FPHA, Hinss2021) harnesses, and score every trace with an untrusted-narrative automated Logbook Judge.   • ⊘0 Inconclusive (0 pts)

Untrusted-Narrative Judging & Why Falsified Claims Earn 2 Points.

Following Encore Science and the ICML 2026 Open Reproductions logbook-judge specification, our automated judge (repro/judge.py) ignores agent prose and evaluates only machine-readable Trackio traces (exit_code == 0, math.isfinite on every tensor metric, and quantitative threshold predicates). Both rigorous verification (Verified / Baseline-Adjusted) and rigorous empirical refutation of an idealized assumption (Falsified) earn 2 points because both resolve the scientific status of the claim on real benchmark data.
